Lecture Abstract

A concise summary of the lecture topic and its core ideas.

This lecture introduces Long Short-Term Memory Networks (LSTMs) as an important extension of recurrent neural networks for processing sequence data. Standard neural networks are effective for fixed-size input-output problems such as image classification, but they lack memory and therefore cannot naturally use earlier context when making later predictions.

To solve this limitation, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) were introduced to process sequences by maintaining a hidden state across time steps. However, simple RNNs often struggle to learn long-term dependencies, especially when the relevant information appears far earlier in the sequence.

LSTMs address this issue with a specialized internal structure based on a cell state and three main gates: the forget gate, the input gate, and the output gate. These mechanisms allow the network to decide what to keep, what to discard, and what to output, making LSTMs highly effective for tasks such as language modeling, translation, sentiment analysis, and sequence prediction.

Key Equations
ft = σ(Wf[ht-1, xt] + bf) (forget gate)
it = σ(Wi[ht-1, xt] + bi) (input gate)
C̃t = tanh(Wc[ht-1, xt] + bc) (new cell state)
Ct = ft * Ct-1 + it * C̃t (update cell state)
ht = ot * tanh(Ct) (generate output and next hidden state)

Blog: Understanding LSTMs in Plain Language

A blog-style article students can read before or after class.

Why LSTMs Matter

Human understanding depends on memory. When we hear or read language, we interpret each word based on the words that came before it. Traditional neural networks do not work this way. They process an input and produce an output, but they do not naturally remember earlier context.

This becomes a major problem for sequence tasks. For example, in language translation, the meaning of a word often depends on earlier words in the sentence. In sentiment analysis, the final sentiment may depend on context built across many tokens. To handle such problems, recurrent neural networks were introduced.

RNNs improve on standard neural networks by passing a hidden state from one time step to the next. This creates a form of memory. However, simple RNNs still struggle when the important information is far in the past. That is the long-term dependency problem.

LSTMs solve this by introducing a dedicated memory pathway called the cell state. Instead of rewriting everything at each step, the network carefully controls what to forget, what new information to add, and what to output. This control happens through gates.

Because of this design, LSTMs became a major breakthrough for sequence modeling and were widely used in natural language processing, speech recognition, and time-series analysis. Even today, they remain an essential concept for understanding how neural networks can model memory.

Key Takeaways

  • Standard neural networks have no built-in memory
  • RNNs process sequences using hidden states
  • Long-term dependencies are hard for simple RNNs
  • LSTMs use gates to control memory flow
  • The cell state is central to the LSTM architecture
Best Mental Model

Think of the cell state as a conveyor belt carrying useful information through time, while the gates act as controllers deciding what information is kept, removed, or revealed.
